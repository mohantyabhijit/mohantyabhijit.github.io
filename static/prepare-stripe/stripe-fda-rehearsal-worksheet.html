UDAIR Speaking Order

For every story, rehearse in this order:

  1. User and stakes: Start with the person, their job, and the consequence of failure.
  2. Diagnosis: State what you initially believed and what evidence changed or sharpened the problem.
  3. Actions and decisions: Explain your decisions, alternatives, tradeoffs, and personal contribution.
  4. Impact: Give the business or behavior change, then the verified metric.
  5. Reflection and reuse: Name what you would change and what became reusable without you.

Do not spend equal time on every section. A strong two-minute answer is approximately 20 seconds on the user, 20 seconds on diagnosis, 50 seconds on decisions, 20 seconds on impact, and 10 seconds on reflection.

Live Simulation One-Page Prompt

Permission Check

Before using AI tools, say:

The recruiter note said tooling was allowed for this simulation. Can I confirm AI tools are permitted in this session?
  • If yes: use your normal environment transparently.
  • If no: continue with workflow mapping, structured prompts, formulas, pseudocode, or a non-AI prototype.

Discover

  • What output are you responsible for?
  • Show me the last real example.
  • Walk me through how it was produced.
  • Who consumes or approves it?
  • Where are time, rework, waiting, and judgment concentrated?
  • What makes the output excellent?
  • What must remain private or human-controlled?
  • What result would make today's session worthwhile?

Map

trigger -> inputs -> transformations -> decisions -> reviewers -> output -> measurement

Choose

State:

The bottleneck appears to be ___. A good result must satisfy ___. I recommend building ___ today because it creates a testable win while preserving human control over ___. Does that match your priority?

Build

  • Use a real artifact.
  • Keep scope narrow.
  • Separate deterministic and generative steps.
  • Add one quality check.
  • Let the marketer provide domain rules.
  • Explain assumptions.

Test

  • Ask the marketer to grade the result.
  • Find one failure.
  • Improve it.
  • Test again if time allows.

Close

  • What works now?
  • What remains human-reviewed?
  • What value could repetition create?
  • What can the marketer do alone next?
  • What is the next experiment?
  • What could be reused by peers?
