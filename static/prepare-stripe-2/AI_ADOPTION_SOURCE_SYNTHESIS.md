# AI Adoption Source Synthesis

Built from Supadata transcripts, Exa research, and the local AI Adoption OS source set.

## Executive Pattern

AI adoption works when it is treated as operating-model change, not tool rollout. Across the sources, the repeatable pattern is:

- Start with a business or customer problem.
- Put AI inside a real workflow, not beside it as optional tooling.
- Make leaders visibly model usage and resource the change.
- Create local champions who demonstrate real work.
- Protect time for learning and experimentation.
- Measure behavior change, cycle time, quality, confidence, and business outcomes.
- Govern with usable guardrails and post-launch monitoring, not only approval committees.
- Scale what works, retire what does not, and reuse capabilities across teams.

For a Stripe Forward Deployed AI Accelerator, Marketing role, the core implication is clear: the job is not to demo AI to marketers. The job is to enter a marketing workflow, find the business bottleneck, coach the team into a new behavior, measure the improvement, and leave behind a repeatable system.

## Cross-Source Themes

### 1. Adoption Is Behavior Change

- People resist when they do not know what AI means for their job, what is allowed, or how it applies to their daily work.
- Generic training creates awareness but rarely changes team behavior.
- Role-specific examples, peer demos, and manager follow-through matter more than abstract AI literacy.
- Leaders need to communicate value repeatedly and model usage themselves.

### 2. Workflow Redesign Beats Tool Access

- Most organizations already have AI access, but few have embedded AI deeply enough into processes to create enterprise-level value.
- The winning move is to redesign handoffs, decision points, review loops, and ownership between humans and AI.
- Agentic systems raise the bar: teams need to orchestrate, evaluate, overrule, and monitor AI rather than just generate content faster.

### 3. Champions Are the Scaling Mechanism

- Local champions are more credible than central AI teams.
- Cohorts, dojos, brown bags, and internal summits work because people learn from peers doing adjacent work.
- Each wave should produce the coaches for the next wave.

### 4. Measurement Must Be Outcome-Led

- Adoption metrics alone are weak. Daily active usage matters, but only when paired with business results.
- Good measures include cycle time, quality, campaign throughput, customer satisfaction, decision confidence, reuse, and reduction of manual handoffs.
- High-performing organizations track KPIs for AI solutions and connect them to growth, innovation, or efficiency.

### 5. Governance Must Enable Learning

- Governance by committee slows adoption.
- No governance creates risk, trust gaps, and shadow usage.
- The useful middle ground is clear guardrails, human validation rules, escalation paths, and monitoring after launch.

## Source 1: Asana - How to Drive AI Adoption Across Your Marketing Team

URL: https://youtu.be/ih2eFFiJTe8  
Local transcript: `ai-adoption-extra-supadata-transcript.json`

### Core Themes

- Marketing teams are repeatedly forced to become "modern marketers"; AI is a larger shift than social/mobile.
- Adoption needs both top-down sponsorship and bottom-up use-case discovery.
- Learning time must be protected inside work, not pushed to nights and weekends.
- Teams should pitch AI ideas that solve customer or business problems.
- Internal AI influencers and councils help spread practical use cases.
- Marketing use cases include strategy, media, creative, content, AEO, regional partner campaigns, and customer-query-driven content.

### Learnings

- If you give people tools without a business problem, they will not know what success means.
- A two-day internal AI summit is a strong adoption pattern:
  - Day 1: protected async learning.
  - Day 2: teams return and pitch AI use cases.
- The best ideas often come from ICs closest to the work.
- Marketing adoption should help people spend more time on relationship-building, strategy, and customer understanding.
- AI can reveal content gaps by showing what customers ask that existing marketing does not answer.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Create a visible AI learning rhythm for the marketing org.
- Make training bimonthly or cohort-based, with experts and internal practitioners.
- Ask every team to start from a customer or business problem.
- Fund small experiments that are tied to measurable marketing outcomes.
- Build an internal influencer council of curious practitioners.
- Share real examples from regional, content, creative, campaign, and lifecycle teams.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not start with "here is a tool, go use it."
- Do not expect people to learn AI outside work.
- Do not make adoption purely top-down; the best workflows are often discovered bottom-up.
- Do not measure success only by tool usage.
- Do not separate AI training from actual marketing planning and campaign work.

### Success Looks Like

- Marketers pitch AI use cases in terms of customer or business problems.
- Teams use AI to increase experiment velocity, content relevance, and campaign throughput.
- ICs become internal examples for peers.
- Regional marketers can scale output while preserving time for strategic partner work.
- Marketing shifts from one big campaign plan to faster beta, test, learn, optimize loops.

### Failure Looks Like

- AI remains a side activity disconnected from planning.
- People attend training but do not change campaign, content, or creative workflows.
- Leadership talks about AI but does not protect time or budget.
- Use cases are tool-led and impossible to evaluate.
- AI adoption becomes another enablement program instead of a new operating rhythm.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- For Stripe marketing teams, run "AI workflow summits" around concrete GTM problems: launch planning, sales enablement, customer proof, lifecycle nurture, partner marketing, and localization.
- Ask teams to bring one workflow and one metric, not one tool request.
- Build a Stripe marketing champion network across product marketing, growth, lifecycle, field marketing, comms, and regional teams.
- Use AI to expose customer-question gaps in existing content, especially around trust, compliance, pricing, onboarding, integrations, and global payments.
- Position yourself as the operator who creates protected learning time, turns ideas into measured experiments, and scales the winners.

## Source 2: Asana - How To Overcome Resistance And Lead AI Adoption In Your Organization

URL: https://youtu.be/BnH-eewmbX4  
Local transcript: `ai-adoption-supadata-transcripts.json`

### Core Themes

- AI adoption is less about technology and more about people readiness.
- Resistance comes from fear, unclear role impact, generic training, and low trust.
- Pilots often become "not dead, not thriving" because they lack a change system.
- Infrastructure, data, and governance can either enable scale or block it.
- Change must move through awareness, mobilization, integration, and transition.

### Learnings

- Employees are often nervous, not hostile.
- People ask practical questions: Will I have a job? How does this fit my role? What does this mean day to day?
- Governance by committee can slow every decision.
- Leaders may be enthusiastic without committing resources or making hard tradeoffs.
- Adoption has to be measured through stakeholder feedback, progress signals, and operational integration.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Diagnose readiness across strategy, people, workflow, infrastructure, and governance.
- Communicate what is changing, why it matters, and how it affects daily work.
- Use role-specific training rather than generic AI education.
- Build trust through examples, boundaries, and repeatable support.
- Measure adoption progress and adjust the change plan.
- Transition ownership into operations once the new behavior becomes part of the rhythm of business.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not assume enthusiasm equals sponsorship.
- Do not run pilots without a backbone for scaling.
- Do not treat nervous employees as blockers.
- Do not bury teams in governance meetings before they can learn.
- Do not let AI adoption stay separate from the operating cadence.

### Success Looks Like

- Teams understand how AI applies to their own work.
- Resistance turns into informed experimentation.
- Pilots either scale or are intentionally retired.
- Governance is clear enough that people can move.
- AI becomes part of operations, culture, and business rhythm.

### Failure Looks Like

- Employees receive generic training and remain unsure how to act.
- Leaders announce AI priorities without budget, time, or decision support.
- Infrastructure cannot support workflow integration.
- Governance blocks all movement or arrives after risky usage has spread.
- Pilots sit in limbo.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- When embedded with Stripe marketing, start with resistance mapping: fear, unclear policy, unclear workflow fit, missing data, and manager hesitation.
- Build adoption plans that include stakeholder feedback and progress measurement.
- Convert "AI anxiety" into a clear role-specific path: what marketers should stop doing, continue doing, and start doing with AI.
- Make governance usable for fintech marketing: approved data, claims review, compliance review, customer confidentiality, and escalation.
- Treat pilot limbo as a failure mode to prevent from day one.

## Source 3: DX - The AI Adoption Playbook: Lessons From Microsoft's Internal Strategy

URL: https://youtu.be/c51ToE4pPpY  
Local transcript: `ai-adoption-supadata-transcripts.json`

### Core Themes

- Hype can be a barrier because it creates unrealistic expectations.
- Developers and technical users are skeptical by nature; they need evidence.
- Leadership advocacy significantly increases daily usage.
- Formal training matters because AI is task-sensitive: it works better for some tasks than others.
- Local champions and peer demos are powerful because people trust practitioners.

### Learnings

- Adoption improves when teams can see real examples from peers.
- Central infrastructure teams are not enough.
- Brown bag sessions, screen shares, and real workflow walkthroughs make AI concrete.
- Mandates may produce compliance, but advocacy plus proof produces usage.
- Measuring usage helps identify where adoption is stuck, but it must be paired with value evidence.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Have leaders visibly advocate for AI and explain why it matters.
- Teach people which tasks AI is good for and which tasks it is not.
- Identify local champions in teams, not only central AI owners.
- Run peer-led demos using real work.
- Measure adoption patterns and use them to target coaching.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not rely only on mandates.
- Do not let hype overpromise what AI can do.
- Do not assume technical teams will adopt without proof.
- Do not send a central team to preach AI without local credibility.
- Do not train on generic prompts without task-fit guidance.

### Success Looks Like

- Daily usage rises because people see concrete value.
- Team-level champions normalize AI in the flow of work.
- Users understand where AI helps and where it does not.
- Skeptics become informed adopters after seeing peer evidence.
- Leaders reinforce adoption without forcing brittle compliance.

### Failure Looks Like

- People try AI on bad-fit tasks and conclude it is useless.
- Hype creates disappointment.
- Central dashboards show low usage but no one knows why.
- Training is disconnected from actual work.
- Local teams wait for permission or proof from someone else.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- Treat Stripe marketers like expert skeptics: show proof in their workflow, not generic demos.
- Build "watch me work" sessions with product marketers, lifecycle marketers, and regional marketers.
- Create a task-fit guide for marketing work: research synthesis, audience briefs, launch messaging, campaign variants, sales enablement, localization, competitive analysis, and compliance-sensitive copy review.
- Use leadership advocacy, but make adoption peer-led at the team level.
- Measure both usage and business value: time to brief, campaign iteration velocity, content reuse, enablement quality, and sales/customer feedback.

## Source 4: AWS Events - A Leader's Guide To Advanced Team Structures In An Agentic World

URL: https://youtu.be/O7u6myBRsns  
Local transcript: `ai-adoption-supadata-transcripts.json`

### Core Themes

- The question has moved from "Should we use AI?" to "How do we organize, govern, and protect what we have while moving faster?"
- Team structure changes as AI enters the workflow.
- The valuable worker becomes an orchestrator: someone who can point agents at problems, evaluate output, steer iterations, and overrule.
- Companies should decide when to consume, compose, or build AI.
- Outcome measurement matters more than controlling every step.
- Agentic systems require governance for multi-agent coordination and risk.

### Learnings

- Specialists need breadth; generalists gain depth through AI.
- Smaller teams of expert generalists plus agents can reduce coordination overhead.
- The craft shifts from building fastest to orchestrating fastest.
- Build custom models only where differentiation justifies cost and speed tradeoffs.
- AI failure modes include poor observability, business drift, agent coordination issues, and teams unable to debug what they cannot see.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Redesign teams around workflows, not narrow lanes.
- Train people to orchestrate, evaluate, and overrule AI output.
- Use "consume, compose, build" as an investment framework.
- Set outcomes and guardrails, then let teams discover the path.
- Build governance for agent interaction, escalation, and accountability.
- Invest in observability so teams can see when AI gets worse.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not hire only for the framework or tool of the year.
- Do not assume specialist silos remain optimal in agentic workflows.
- Do not build custom models when managed or composed solutions are enough.
- Do not manage every step while leaving outcomes vague.
- Do not deploy agents without observability and escalation design.

### Success Looks Like

- Teams own workflows end to end.
- Agents fill specialist gaps while humans preserve judgment and accountability.
- Leaders loosen process control but tighten outcome measurement.
- Build decisions are reserved for real differentiation.
- Governance handles agent coordination before incidents occur.

### Failure Looks Like

- AI pilots fail because operators cannot debug invisible behavior.
- Agents operate without clear escalation.
- Teams keep old handoffs and simply add AI tooling.
- Leaders over-control execution and under-measure outcomes.
- The organization builds expensive AI where it should have composed or consumed.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- In Stripe marketing, help teams become "expert generalist" operators who can orchestrate research, content, segmentation, compliance review, and launch workflows.
- Use consume/compose/build to guide AI tooling for marketing:
  - Consume for generic productivity.
  - Compose for Stripe-specific GTM workflows.
  - Build only where Stripe has durable differentiation, data, or risk needs.
- Define human review points for regulated marketing claims.
- Measure outcomes like launch cycle time, campaign quality, review latency, and customer-facing accuracy.
- Position agentic AI as workflow orchestration, not autonomous marketing.

## Source 5: McKinsey - The State Of AI: Global Survey 2025

URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai

### Core Themes

- AI use is widespread, but scaling remains limited.
- Nearly two-thirds of organizations have not begun scaling AI across the enterprise.
- AI agents are being explored, but agentic scaling is still narrow.
- High performers use AI for growth and innovation, not only efficiency.
- Workflow redesign is one of the strongest factors tied to meaningful business impact.
- Senior leadership ownership and role modeling distinguish high performers.

### Learnings

- Broad usage does not equal enterprise value.
- AI high performers are more likely to redesign workflows, scale agents, invest more, and use management practices across strategy, talent, operating model, technology, data, adoption, and scaling.
- Tracking KPIs and embedding AI into business processes matter.
- Human validation rules for model outputs are part of high-performance practice.
- Marketing and sales are among the functions where revenue increases from AI are commonly reported.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Move beyond experimentation into enterprise scaling practices.
- Set growth and innovation objectives, not just efficiency targets.
- Redesign individual workflows around AI.
- Have senior leaders demonstrate ownership and role model use.
- Track KPIs for AI solutions.
- Define when human validation is required.
- Invest in talent, technology, data infrastructure, and adoption mechanisms.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not confuse regular AI use with scaled value.
- Do not run fragmented use cases without workflow integration.
- Do not set only cost-reduction goals.
- Do not leave adoption without a C-level owner or senior leadership role modeling.
- Do not ignore AI risks such as inaccuracy, privacy, explainability, regulatory compliance, and IP.

### Success Looks Like

- AI is scaled across functions and embedded in business processes.
- Workflows are redesigned, not merely accelerated.
- Business impact is visible in growth, innovation, customer satisfaction, competitive differentiation, and efficiency.
- Leaders own adoption and model behavior.
- Human validation and risk mitigation are explicit.

### Failure Looks Like

- AI remains in pilots despite broad interest.
- EBIT impact stays low because work has not changed.
- Adoption is measured by activity rather than business outcomes.
- Agent experiments stay isolated in one or two functions.
- Risks materialize faster than mitigation practices.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- For Stripe, frame AI adoption around growth, customer understanding, and GTM quality, not only marketer productivity.
- Build KPI trees for marketing AI workflows: lead quality, conversion, activation, launch velocity, content accuracy, localization speed, sales enablement reuse, and customer trust.
- Use senior marketing leaders as visible adopters and sponsors.
- Embed human validation in workflows where claims, compliance, pricing, financial services language, or customer data are involved.
- Tie every AI pilot to a scaling path and business metric from the start.

## Source 6: MIT Sloan Management Review - Create Generative AI Value At Scale

URL: https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/create-generative-ai-value-at-scale/

### Core Themes

- General-purpose LLM access is not enough to create strategic value.
- Meaningful ROI depends on process improvements that scale across functions and business units.
- Leaders who scale value do three things:
  - Expand use cases across processes, not isolated tasks.
  - Treat each use case as a work in progress.
  - Quickly abandon use cases that fail to bring measurable value.
- Traditional multidivisional organizations struggle because knowledge and resources are fragmented.
- The "AI spine" is a cross-functional structure for implementing, evolving, and abandoning LLM use cases at scale.

### Learnings

- AI value creation requires organizational structure, not just technology.
- Cross-functional sharing helps teams spot reusable process improvements.
- Disciplined governance keeps resources focused on high-impact opportunities.
- The ability to kill weak use cases is part of scaling.
- A hub-and-spoke model may not be enough if it does not create shared learning across units.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Build an AI spine: a flexible cross-functional core that connects business users, technical experts, governance, and implementation.
- Focus on process-level improvements.
- Continuously improve use cases after launch.
- Share patterns across business units.
- Make abandon/continue/scale decisions based on measurable value.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not distribute LLM access and assume strategy will emerge.
- Do not optimize isolated tasks while ignoring end-to-end process value.
- Do not let business units compete for AI resources without shared learning.
- Do not keep low-value use cases alive for political reasons.
- Do not use governance only to approve; use it to focus and prune.

### Success Looks Like

- AI use cases improve processes across teams.
- Reusable patterns emerge.
- Use cases evolve through feedback.
- Weak experiments are killed quickly.
- A cross-functional AI spine helps scale what works.

### Failure Looks Like

- LLM usage remains personal productivity only.
- Process improvements are trapped in one unit.
- Teams duplicate similar AI work.
- No one owns portfolio focus.
- Low-value pilots consume resources indefinitely.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- Build a marketing AI spine for Stripe: product marketing, growth, lifecycle, sales, legal/compliance, data, design, and regional teams.
- Use it to move learnings across campaign, launch, localization, partner, and lifecycle workflows.
- Create a shared library of workflow patterns, prompts, evaluation rubrics, approved claims, customer objections, and reusable GTM assets.
- Run monthly scale/kill reviews for marketing AI experiments.
- Make your role the connective tissue that turns local wins into reusable operating capability.

## Source 7: AI Engineer / Accenture - Most Enterprise Agentic Projects Are Doomed, Here's Why

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AGkzpxMdPn8  
Local transcript: `ai-adoption-agentic-projects-supadata-transcript.json`

### Core Themes

- Building an agentic app can be fast; getting it to production in an enterprise can take far longer.
- The hard part is alignment across infrastructure, security, AI gateway, data governance, application teams, and finance.
- Approval infrastructure was designed for human speed, not AI-speed delivery.
- Trust should graduate through shadow mode, advisory mode, and controlled autonomy.
- Delivery should be hypothesis-driven and evidence-based.
- Finance should fund a portfolio of bets rather than demand certainty from every project.

### Learnings

- Enterprise AI failure is often organizational, not technical.
- Human approval chains need to become executable controls and policies where possible.
- Outcome evidence matters more than project-plan completion.
- Small loops of build, evaluate, iterate are better than milestone-heavy delivery.
- Production trust grows gradually as confidence in outcomes increases.

### How To Drive AI Adoption

- Start with hypotheses and define what evidence would increase confidence.
- Use small delivery loops with evaluation built in.
- Align security, data, infrastructure, product, and finance early.
- Use staged autonomy:
  - Shadow mode: AI observes or runs in parallel.
  - Advisory mode: AI recommends and humans approve.
  - Controlled autonomy: AI acts within bounded guardrails.
- Convert approval policies into executable checks where possible.
- Fund a portfolio and rebalance based on evidence.

### How Not To Do It

- Do not treat production blockers as code issues only.
- Do not wait until the end to involve security, governance, infrastructure, or finance.
- Do not graduate autonomy based on calendar milestones.
- Do not demand fixed ROI certainty from every early AI bet.
- Do not keep approval systems at human speed while expecting AI-speed delivery.

### Success Looks Like

- Teams can move from prototype to production through clear evidence gates.
- Trust increases because the system proves itself in lower-risk modes first.
- Governance, security, and infrastructure are part of delivery, not late-stage blockers.
- Finance treats AI as a managed portfolio.
- The organization learns faster because evaluations are part of every iteration.

### Failure Looks Like

- A two-week prototype takes a year to ship because stakeholders were not aligned.
- AI remains stuck in pilot because no one can approve production risk.
- Autonomy is either blocked forever or granted too quickly.
- Project plans show progress but outcome confidence does not improve.
- Teams optimize demos instead of deployment paths.

### Recommendations For Stripe Role

- Stripe is a high-trust, regulated environment, so use staged autonomy for marketing AI:
  - Shadow: AI drafts briefs, analyzes customer questions, or reviews content without publishing.
  - Advisory: AI recommends campaign variants, sales enablement gaps, or lifecycle segments for human approval.
  - Controlled: AI operates bounded internal workflows with audit trails and review gates.
- Bring legal, compliance, data, brand, and marketing ops into the workflow early.
- Define evidence gates for every marketing AI pilot.
- Treat each pilot as a portfolio bet: continue, scale, or stop.
- Make the production path part of the first conversation, not an afterthought.

## Stripe Role Operating Playbook

### How To Drive AI Adoption At Stripe

- Enter through a real marketing workflow, not a tool demo.
- Ask: What decision, handoff, asset, audience, or launch motion is slow or low quality today?
- Map the current workflow with users.
- Identify where AI can help:
  - Research synthesis.
  - Customer insight mining.
  - Message testing.
  - Campaign variant generation.
  - Sales enablement production.
  - Localization.
  - Content gap discovery.
  - Compliance-sensitive review preparation.
- Start with one bounded pilot.
- Use staged trust: shadow, advisory, controlled autonomy.
- Build champions inside the team.
- Measure outcomes and behavior change.
- Package the pattern so another team can reuse it.

### How Not To Do It At Stripe

- Do not lead with "AI can do X."
- Do not assume marketers lack curiosity; assume they lack time, proof, and safe workflow paths.
- Do not propose generic prompt training as the main intervention.
- Do not skip legal, compliance, brand, privacy, or data constraints.
- Do not let pilots drift without scale or kill criteria.
- Do not separate AI adoption from marketing planning, campaign execution, and GTM reviews.

### What Success Should Sound Like In Interviews

- "I would start by finding a high-value marketing workflow where AI can change cycle time, quality, or decision confidence."
- "I would work with the team closest to the work, because the best use cases often come bottom-up."
- "I would protect learning time and turn it into applied output, such as use-case pitches or workflow prototypes."
- "I would create a champion loop so early adopters teach the next cohort."
- "I would define human validation and compliance checkpoints before scaling."
- "I would measure adoption through business outcomes, not just usage."

### Strong Example Framing For The FDA Accelerator Role

- User problem: Marketing team is under pressure to launch faster, personalize more, and maintain trust/compliance.
- Intervention: Embed with the team, map workflow, identify AI leverage points, run a staged pilot, and build champion capacity.
- Behavior change: Marketers move from manual asset production to AI-assisted research, synthesis, review prep, and campaign iteration.
- Durable outcome: Reusable playbook, approved guardrails, measured lift, champion network, and a scale/kill mechanism.
