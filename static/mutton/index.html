Mutton with Potatoes

A deeply browned, spice-paste curry with tender meat, crisp-edged potatoes, and a glossy gravy.

Serves a family · Pressure-cooker finish

The character

The key is the long bhuna: brown the onions, cook the fresh spice paste until the oil separates, then reduce the mutton in two stages before pressure-cooking.

For 500 g

Marinate 10–15 min · bhuna on high 10 min · bhuna covered 10 min · tomato and potatoes 2 min · 2 whistles high + 1 whistle medium.

Ingredients

  • Mutton — 500 g, washed and drained
  • Turmeric — ½ small tsp for marinade + ½ small tsp for masala
  • Salt — for marinade and later to taste
  • Mustard oil — ½ tbsp for marinade; about 25 ml for cooking
  • Onions — 125 g, sliced lengthwise
  • Potatoes — 2 medium, large pieces, skin on
  • Bay leaf — 1
  • Cinnamon — 1 small piece for tempering; 1–2 pieces for paste
  • Cloves — 2 for tempering; 2 for paste
  • Cardamom — 1 for tempering; 1 for paste
  • Cumin seeds — 1 tbsp
  • Coriander seeds — 1 tbsp
  • Dried red chillies — 2, to taste
  • Black peppercorns — 5–8
  • Star anise — ¼ piece
  • Mace — ¼ piece
  • Nutmeg — a pinch
  • Ginger — ½ inch
  • Garlic — cloves from ½ large bulb
  • Tomato — 1 medium, chopped
  • Hot water — enough to submerge the meat; 2–3 tbsp for the paste

Method

1

Marinate the mutton

Wash and thoroughly drain 500 g mutton. Mix it with ½ small teaspoon turmeric, a little salt, and ½ tablespoon mustard oil. Rest for 10–15 minutes.

2

Prepare the onions

Slice 125 g onions lengthwise. Reserve about half an onion for the spice paste and loosen the remaining layers by hand.

3

Brown the potatoes

Heat about 25 ml mustard oil in a heavy pan. Fry the large, skin-on potato pieces over high heat until well browned and slightly crisp. Remove and set aside.

4

Temper and brown

In the same oil, briefly fry 1 bay leaf, 1 small piece cinnamon, 2 cloves, and 1 cardamom pod. Add the remaining onions and fry over medium heat until properly brown/golden.

5

Grind the fresh spice paste

Grind the dry spices: cumin, coriander, dried chillies, black pepper, star anise, mace, cardamom, nutmeg, cloves, and cinnamon. Add the garlic, ginger, and reserved onion. Add a little water and blend smooth.

6

Cook the masala

Lower the flame fully. Add ½ small teaspoon Kashmiri red-chilli powder and ½ small teaspoon turmeric; stir gently so they do not burn. Add the spice paste, rinse the jar with 2–3 tablespoons water, and add that too. Cover and cook on low to medium for 3–4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until oil separates.

7

Bhuna the mutton

Add the marinated mutton. Cook uncovered on high heat for about 10 minutes. Then cover, reduce to low-medium, and cook for another 10 minutes. The meat’s fat should release and the masala should look glossy and oily.

8

Add tomato and potatoes

Add salt to taste, then add the chopped tomato and fried potatoes. Cook on medium-high for about 2 minutes, until the tomato softens. Do not add the potatoes earlier or they may break down.

9

Pressure-cook

Transfer to a pressure cooker. Add hot water only until the meat is submerged. Cook on high for 2 whistles, reduce to medium, and cook for 1 more whistle—3 whistles total. The exact count may vary with the mutton.

10

Rest and serve

Let the cooker cool completely before opening. The meat should be tender but intact, with a thick, glossy gravy. It will thicken further as it cools. Serve hot.

Cook’s note: The video’s defining technique is the two-stage bhuna—10 minutes uncovered on high, followed by 10 minutes covered on low-medium—before the pressure cooker.
